# Halo 605: output 119 vs output 125 inside R_e

**Short answer:** 605 does form stars in bursts, but neither output catches one in progress. Output 119 is the gas-rich state just before stars form; output 125 is after it, with the central gas cleared out and ionised.

## The two outputs

| | output_00119 | output_00125 |
|---|---|---|
| Redshift | 0.0526 | 0 |
| Cosmic time | 13.070 Gyr | 13.802 Gyr |
| R_e (projected, V band) | 271 pc | 278 pc |
| R_e (3D) | 334 pc | 345 pc |

- The outputs are 732 Myr apart.
- galaxy.json has half-mass radii but no half-light radius, so R_e was computed with pynbody `half_light_r(..., cylindrical=True)` (the same call as in bearings) on stars inside R200c, projected along the box z axis.
- Re-measuring 119 inside 278 pc, so both outputs cover the same volume, changes nothing materially.

## The 113 new stars (IDs in 125 but not in 119, all 288 domains checked)

- Mass: 33,900 Msun at birth, 25,450 Msun now.
- They formed between 37 and 625 Myr after output 119, so every one is older than 100 Myr at output 125.
- Formation came in discrete episodes. The biggest was 56 particles within about 8 Myr, roughly 185 Myr before output 125. Mean star formation rate over the interval: 4.6e-5 Msun/yr.
- Birth densities (from the run's own SF_log): 400 to 1,740 H/cc, all above the run's star-formation threshold of 300. That is 23 to 100 times denser than any gas present in output 119.
- At output 125, 112 of the 113 sit inside R_e, in gas that is now about 0.007 cm^-3 and around 12,000 K.

## Spatial correlation

- At 119 the H2 sits in a dense central structure, and the ionising field has a hole exactly there, because that dense gas shields itself.
- Cell by cell, the ionised fraction rises and the H2 fraction falls as the local field strengthens.
- At 125 every cell sits at the strong-field, low-H2 end. At fixed density the ionising field is still about 16 times higher at 125, so the change is not only lost shielding.

## Against the hypothesis

- **Supports a bursty dwarf:** the star formation history is a string of spikes separated by gaps of 100 to 300 Myr.
- **Does not support "one output is mid-burst":** 119 is quiescent only in that its youngest star is 125 Myr old. It is gas-rich and about to form stars (the first new star appears 37 Myr later). 125 is post-burst.
- Catching the burst itself would need an output at about 13.6 to 13.7 Gyr.

## Caveats

- Birth sites of the new stars can't be placed: SF_log rounds positions to roughly 1 kpc, so only their positions at 125 are plotted.
- The source of the stronger ionising field at 125 is unresolved. It peaks on the stellar centre, yet every star there is at least 106 Myr old. Settling it needs the run's radiation-source settings, which haven't been checked.
- pynbody warns that RAMSES stores radiation densities "in flux units" (pynbody issue 542). The bearings reading was used, so absolute values carry that caveat; the 119-vs-125 ratios don't.
- Projected R_e is about 15% smaller viewed along the box y axis than along x or z.
- Star particles inside 278 pc drop from 4,967 to 4,453 despite the new stars, so older stars moved outward. Not investigated.
- The 1.6 Msun of H2 at 125 is small enough that the earlier per-star H2 artefact matters; the 1,540 Msun at 119 is well above it.
- The halo605_survey_*.json files are no longer in the tree; surveys/Halo605_RT/output_*/ was used instead.
